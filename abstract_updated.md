# Abstract (Updated)

Lignin-based polyurethanes (PUs) represent a compelling route towards sustainable material development, yet the rational design of chemical formulations with targeted thermomechanical properties—such as the glass transition temperature (Tg)—remains a significant challenge. In this work, we present a systematic approach to explore four key structural parameters—lignin content, co-polyol chain length, isocyanate type and functionality, and the [NCO]/[OH] mixing ratio—across 136 unique formulations, constructing a diverse experimental dataset of lignin-based PUs. Feature selection through principal component analysis and a wrapper method identified seven optimal input features, with the swelling ratio—a characterisation output—emerging as a particularly influential predictor. By harnessing this small dataset, we develop a stacking ensemble model that combines five base learners (GBR, Random Forest, SVR, Lasso, and ElasticNet) with Ridge regression as a meta-learner, achieving a mean absolute error of 6.66°C and outperforming all individual base models on the validation set. Model interpretability is enhanced through bivariate kernel density mapping of over 4 million novel formulation combinations, revealing complex non-monotonic structure–property relationships between lignin content, co-polyol molecular weight, stoichiometric ratio and Tg. An adaptive grid search algorithm further enables target-Tg-driven optimisation, efficiently navigating the high-dimensional formulation space within practical computational constraints. The ensemble model's behaviour at extrapolation boundaries is characterised, and its integration into a parallel coordinates user interface is demonstrated, enabling experimental chemists to identify synthesis formulations for desired Tg values without requiring in-depth knowledge of machine learning techniques. This study deepens the understanding of structure–property relationships in lignin-based PUs and provides a scalable, ML-driven tool for the precise design of sustainable materials, highlighting the transformative potential of artificial intelligence in green chemistry and materials innovation.
