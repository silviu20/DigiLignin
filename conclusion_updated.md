# Conclusion (Updated)

Our analysis confirmed that lignin content is the dominant contributor to enhancing the glass transition temperature (Tg) of lignin-based polyurethanes (PUs), a relationship grounded in the increased crosslinking density and π-π stacking interactions introduced by its aromatic structure. Co-polyol content exhibited a complex, non-monotonic relationship with Tg, displaying an overall negative correlation but demonstrating a positive influence at high concentrations relative to other features, consistent with a competing soft-segment/hard-segment phase behaviour. The [NCO]/[OH] ratio acted as a threshold-sensitive parameter: substoichiometric formulations consistently yielded higher and more confident Tg predictions compared to superstoichiometric ones, suggesting that excess hydroxyl groups contribute more favourably to network formation in these lignin-based PU systems. The swelling ratio emerged as a critical characterisation parameter, exhibiting the highest maximum absolute loading in the principal component analysis and consistently improving model performance when included as an input feature. These findings underscore the importance of comprehensive material characterisation—beyond synthesis parameters alone—in developing accurate predictive models for lignin-based PUs.

A systematic dataset of 136 unique lignin-based PU formulations was constructed by varying four key structural parameters: lignin content, co-polyol chain length (PTHF Mn = 250, 650 and 1000 g/mol), isocyanate type and functionality (HDI and HDIt), and the [NCO]/[OH] mixing ratio. Feature selection through principal component analysis and a wrapper method identified seven optimal input features, with Lignin (wt%), Co-polyol type (PTHF), Ratio, Co-polyol (wt%) and Isocyanate (wt%) forming the core predictive set. The stacking ensemble—combining GBR, Random Forest, SVR, Lasso, and ElasticNet base models with Ridge regression as meta-learner—outperformed all individual base models, achieving a validation MAE of 13.41°C and a training MAE of 3.92°C at 10 base estimators, with an overall best reported MAE of 6.66°C. Ensemble learning performance was shown to vary with the number of base estimators: lower counts (~10) produced the most favourable validation metrics, with higher estimator counts introducing overfitting, as reflected by a generalisability gap of 0.38 between validation and training MAE.

Mapping over 4 million novel formulation combinations through systematic feature-space exploration provided detailed insights into structure–property relationships, corroborating the experimental observations regarding lignin content, co-polyol type and stoichiometric ratio. An adaptive grid search algorithm was subsequently implemented to enable target-Tg-driven optimisation, efficiently navigating the high-dimensional formulation space while remaining within practical computational constraints. The model demonstrated restricted extrapolation capabilities, with predictions plateauing at approximately 5.6°C at the lower boundary and converging toward ~79.4°C at the upper boundary—behaviour characteristic of tree-based ensemble methods and consistent with documented limitations in the literature. For visualisation and end-user adoption, parallel coordinates proved to be a particularly effective interface for this dataset, given the relatively small number of features and the practical need to communicate multidimensional formulation–property relationships to experimental chemists.

Returning to the central research question, we have demonstrated that ML models, in combination with domain expertise, can effectively predict and optimise the formulations of sustainable, lignin-based PUs from limited experimental datasets. Despite the promising results, several limitations must be acknowledged. The relatively small experimental dataset (136 samples) may constrain the models' ability to capture the full complexity of lignin PU synthesis space, and the observed overfitting highlights the need for further optimisation of ensemble techniques under low-data regimes. Future research should focus on expanding the experimental dataset by incorporating a wider range of lignin sources, synthesis conditions, and characterisation parameters to improve model robustness and generalisation. Advanced feature engineering, including non-linear transformations and interaction terms, should be investigated to better capture the complex relationships within the data. The integration of physics-informed models with data-driven approaches could leverage domain knowledge and improve extrapolation capabilities, further advancing the design of sustainable polyurethane materials with tailored thermomechanical properties.
